# Kinases vs Globins

A **kinase** is an enzyme (specifically a phosphotransferase). Its biological function is to catalyze the transfer of a terminal phosphate group (gamma-phosphate) from a high-energy molecule, typically ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate), to a specific target molecule (a substrate), through phosphorylation.

A **globin** is a transport and storage metalloprotein. Its biological function is the reversible binding of diatomic gases, primarily molecular oxygen, through coordinate covalent bonding. Key for this is the existence of a prosthetic group. Standard examples include hemoglobin (in blood) and myoglobin (in muscle).
