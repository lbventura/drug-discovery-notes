- [13] sp3 hybridized orbitals and sigma bonds (Methane CH_4): sigma bonds are the strongest covalent bonds since the overlap is along the same axis. Rotation along the bond axis is possible.
- [14] sp2 hybridized orbitals and pi bonds; formed when double or triple bonds are formed on top of sigma bonds (Ethyne C_2 H_2). The pi bond makes the double bond rigid.
- [15] Newman projections: eclipsed and staggered conformations of the ethane molecule have different energy levels. Dihedral angle (of 60 and 0 degrees). Due to the repulsion of the electron clouds, the staggered conformation is more energetically stable
- [16] Newman projections 2: Butane (C_4 H_10) anti-conformation (dihedral angle of 180 degrees between CH3) is the most stable as CH_3 is as far as possible from each other.
- [17] Chair and boat representations of Cyclohexane (C_6H_12): axial (vertical) and equatorial (horizontal) hydrogens. Chair is more stable since the CH_2 are further apart.
- [18] Double Newman diagram for methylcyclohexane: how axial and equatorial configurations differ when a Methyl group is introduced; the equatorial is stabler because the separation angles are greater
- [19] Introduction to Chirality: chiral molecules are those that cannot be superimposed on their mirror image. Chiral atoms can cause chiral asymmetries: usually C bonded to 4 groups. However, if there are two chiral centers, the molecule can still be non-chiral (see [21]).
