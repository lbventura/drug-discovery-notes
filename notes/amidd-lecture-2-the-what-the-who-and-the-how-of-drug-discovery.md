## Drug modalities

- Small molecules (Molecular weight of ~O(100) Da (Dalton))
- Peptides (MW ~ O(10^3) Da)
- Oligonucleotides (MW ~ O*(10^3 - 10^5) Da)
-- siRNA (small interfering RNA) 
- Proteins (MW ~ O(10^6) Da)
-- Monoclonal antibody

## Risks and costs associated with each stage of the linear view of drug discovery

Target-to-hit -> hit-to-lead -> lead-optimization -> pre-clinical -> phase 1 -> phase 2 -> phase 3 -> submission to launch

The most cost-intensive phases are the lead optimization, followed by phases 2, 3, 1, respectively, and hit-to-lead and preclinical optimization.

Lead optimization has the longest cycle time in discovery, whereas phases 2 and 3 have the longest cycle times in development.

### Target-to-hit

Once a biological target is validated as a driver of a disease, the goal is to find a small molecule or biologic compound that successfully binds to it and modulates its function.
**A "Hit" is simply a molecule that demonstrates confirmed activity against the target above a certain statistical threshold in an assay.** At this stage, the molecule doesn't need to be a drug; it just needs to prove that it *interacts* with the target.

### Hit-to-lead

A "Hit" is usually a crude, flawed compound: It might bind to the target, but it might also be highly toxic, totally insoluble, or easily destroyed. The goal of H2L is to take the best hit series and systematically alter their chemical structures to fix these flaws, transforming them into a "Lead" compound.
A "Lead" is a prototypical drug molecule. It is potent, selective, and has the foundational pharmacokinetic properties required to be tested in live animal models.

## Conclusions

1. Small molecules, proteins and oligonucleotides are common modalities of drugs,
2. Major players in the game of drug discovery have distinct interests and concerns. They interact and give feedback to each other to identify new drugs.
